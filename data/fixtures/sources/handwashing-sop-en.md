# Standard Operating Procedure: Handwashing for Food Handlers

Restaurant kitchen — all food handling staff. Synthetic training document.

## Purpose

Clean hands stop germs from spreading to food. Every food handler must wash their hands using
this procedure.

## When to wash your hands

- Before starting work and after every break.
- Before handling ready-to-eat food.
- After handling raw meat, poultry or fish.
- After using the toilet, coughing, sneezing or touching your face or hair.
- After taking out the rubbish or cleaning.

## Before you start

Remove rings and watches. Roll your sleeves up to the elbow. Use the hand-wash sink only — never
wash your hands in a food preparation sink.

## Procedure

1. Wet your hands with warm running water.
2. Apply liquid soap from the dispenser.
3. Scrub all surfaces of your hands for at least 20 seconds: palms, backs of the hands, between the
   fingers, thumbs, fingertips and under the nails.
4. Rinse your hands under running water, with the fingertips pointing down.
5. Dry your hands with a single-use paper towel. Use the towel to turn off the tap.

## Do not

- Do not dry your hands on your apron or on a cloth towel.
- Never touch ready-to-eat food with bare hands; use clean gloves or utensils.

## Gloves

Gloves do not replace handwashing. Wash your hands before putting on gloves. Change gloves when
they are torn, after handling raw meat, and at least every 4 hours during continuous use.
